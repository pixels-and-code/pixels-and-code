<Kicker>The problem</Kicker>

## Hundreds of hours, in the wrong order

Actual plays are podcasts of people playing tabletop roleplaying games: Critical Role, Dimension 20, Worlds Beyond Number. One story runs for years, and a single episode often lasts three or four hours.

Podcast apps treat them like news. Every campaign, one-shot and bonus episode lands in one list, newest first. Starting a campaign at episode one means scrolling back through hundreds of episodes and keeping track of where you are yourself.

Rolecast fixes that. It splits each show into its campaigns, plays them in order, and remembers where you are in each one.

<Kicker>Research</Kicker>

## Reading the feeds first

I started by downloading 22 actual-play feeds and measuring what they actually contain.

Apple's season tag looks like the answer, but only 12 shows use it consistently. Some stopped partway through a campaign, and in five shows a season is not a campaign at all. Not one show gives its seasons a name.

The only signal every show uses is the episode title.

<Kicker>Grouping</Kicker>

## The title names the campaign

Every show has its own title convention, but one rule held across them: split the title on its separators, find the part that carries the episode number, and that part names the campaign.

I prototyped the grouper in Python against the real feeds, then rebuilt it in Swift, in a package with no interface code and 60 tests. It combines titles with season tags where a show uses them reliably, and keeps bonus and talk episodes apart from the story.

<Kicker>Fixes without a release</Kicker>

## Rules that update themselves

Some shows need help the grouper cannot guess: older campaigns that live on a separate feed, or a series whose titles name it badly. Those fixes live in one JSON file, served from this website.

The app checks the file every time it opens. When nothing has changed, the server answers "not modified" and that is the end of it. When something has, any show you have open regroups straight away. A fix reaches everyone the next time they open the app, without waiting for App Review.

A command-line tool runs every change against the live feeds, using the app's own grouping code, before I publish it.

```json
{
  "name": "Critical Role",
  "feeds": ["https://feeds.simplecast.com/LXz4Q9rJ"],
  "longCampaignEpisodes": 30,
  "extraFeeds": [
    {
      "url": "https://feeds.megaphone.fm/DYEAN7027366551",
      "include": ["^Vox Machina Ep\\. \\d+", "^C2E\\d+\\b"],
      "comesFirst": true
    }
  ]
}
```

<Kicker>Listening</Kicker>

## Built for four-hour episodes

Episodes can run to 400 MB, and many hosts insert adverts as the file downloads. That breaks streaming and resumed downloads, so Rolecast downloads whole episodes in the background before it plays them.

Downloads wait for Wi-Fi unless you allow mobile data, and the app asks before it uses any. The next episodes of each campaign you have started stay downloaded, and played ones clear themselves.

The player takes its background from the show's artwork, and has a sleep timer, playback speed, and buttons to skip back 15 seconds or forward 30.

<Kicker>Everywhere</Kicker>

## iPhone, iPad, CarPlay and iCloud

On iPad, the player stays open beside whatever you are browsing: the library, a show or a campaign. In the car, CarPlay has its own tabs for shows and downloads, and you can browse a show by campaign.

Shows you follow, your progress and what is playing sync through iCloud, using Apple's CloudKit sync engine and your own private database. There is no account to create, and nothing passes through a server of mine.

<Kicker>Discover</Kicker>

## Find shows by the game they play

Discover lists actual plays and fiction podcasts, tagged by the game being played. The tags come from the same rules file, so new shows and systems appear without an update.

<Kicker>Shipping</Kicker>

## From simulator to App Store

The App Store screenshots come from one command. UI tests load a demo library, drive the app on iPhone and iPad simulators, and a script composes the finished images.

Rolecast collects no data. There is no analytics, advertising or tracking, and its App Store privacy label says "Data Not Collected".
